<p class="tldr"><strong>The short answer:</strong> a social media consultant audits your accounts and hands you a strategy to carry out, usually billed hourly or as a flat project fee. A social media manager does the ongoing work (planning, creating, posting, replying) on a monthly retainer. If you have the time but not the plan, start with a consultant; if you have the plan but not the time, hire a manager.</p>

People often search for one when they need the other. A business owner who says "I need a social media consultant" frequently means "I need someone to take this off my plate," and that's a manager's job. Here's how to tell the two roles apart, and which one fits where you are.

## What does a freelance social media consultant do?

A consultant gives you direction. They look at where you are, decide where you should be, and write it down so you or your team can follow it. A typical engagement includes:

- An audit of your current accounts and what's been working
- Your audience and how you want to be positioned
- Three or four content themes (pillars) and the platforms worth your time
- A plan for the next 90 days, often with a sample month
- Training or a walkthrough so your team can carry it out

The deliverable is advice and a plan, not a stream of finished posts.

## What does a social media manager do?

A manager runs the work month after month. That usually means a monthly content calendar, design, captions, scheduling, replies to comments and messages, and a short report on how things went. You're paying for steady execution, so the fee is typically a retainer. For typical ranges, see [freelance social media manager rates](../freelance-social-media-manager-cost-us/).

## What's the difference between a consultant and a manager?

The difference comes down to who does the work after the plan exists. This table summarizes it:

| | Consultant | Manager |
|---|---|---|
| What you get | Audit, strategy, written plan | Finished posts, scheduling, replies, reporting |
| Who does the work | You or your team | The freelancer |
| Typical engagement | One-off project or a few sessions | Ongoing, month to month |
| Usual pricing | Hourly, flat project fee or small advisory retainer | Monthly retainer |
| Best for | Owners with time but no plan | Owners with a plan but no time |
| What you still do | Make and post everything | Approve content and answer the questions only you can |

## How much does a social media consultant charge per hour?

There's no official rate card, and I'd be wary of any article that claims one. Consultants generally price in one of four ways:

- **Hourly:** suits short calls and open-ended advice.
- **Flat project fee:** a set price for a defined deliverable, such as an audit and 90-day plan.
- **Strategy session:** a paid half-day or full-day working session.
- **Monthly advisory retainer:** a small fee for regular check-ins while your team does the work.

For a sense of scale, the [SolidGigs rate guide](https://solidgigs.com/blog/freelance-social-media-manager-rates/) puts experts with five or more years' experience at $75 to $150 or more an hour. That guide is about managers, but strategy work is typically priced toward the senior end of any range, because you're paying for judgment rather than production. It's a vendor-published figure, so use it as a sanity check, not a benchmark.

A flat fee is easier to budget than an hourly rate, so ask for one where you can. Independent consultants are usually 1099 contractors, and the same paperwork applies as for managers, covered in [how to hire a freelance social media manager](../how-to-hire-a-freelance-social-media-manager-us/).

## Which one does your business need?

Match the role to your actual gap:

- **You have time but no plan:** hire a consultant, then run the plan yourself.
- **You have a plan but no time:** hire a manager.
- **You're launching or rebranding:** a short consulting phase first, then a manager to keep it going. A craft brewery opening a taproom is a good example.
- **You work in a regulated field:** either can work, but build in an approval step so a qualified person checks every post. A home-remodeling company has fewer rules than a financial advisor.
- **Your budget is very tight:** book one consultant session, then do it yourself using a simple [content plan](../social-media-content-plan-small-business/). An e-commerce candle brand with one founder is a typical case.

## What should you ask a consultant before you hire one?

Because the deliverable is a document and some advice, the questions differ from those for a manager:

- **What exactly will I receive?** A written plan, a recorded walkthrough, a follow-up call?
- **Is the plan specific to my business?** A recycled template with your logo on it isn't a strategy.
- **How much support follows the handover?** Some consultants include a check-in a month later; others stop at delivery.
- **Who implements it?** If the answer is you, be honest about whether you have the hours.

## What should a social media strategy include?

Whoever writes it, a useful plan covers these points:

1. **Goals you can measure.** "More inquiries from Instagram" is measurable. "Be more visible" isn't.
2. **Your audience.** Who they are and what they ask about.
3. **Three or four content pillars.** The themes you'll keep returning to.
4. **Platforms and frequency.** Where you'll post and how often, realistically.
5. **Formats.** Single images, carousels, short video, stories.
6. **A sample month.** At least one planned month so you can see it working.
7. **An approval process.** Who checks posts, and how fast.
8. **Disclosure rules.** If you work with creators or send free products, the FTC expects a clear "ad" or "sponsored" label.
9. **What to measure.** Saves, shares, replies and messages usually say more than likes.

Nobody can promise results, because they depend on your offer, budget and market. A good plan makes your effort more focused; it doesn't guarantee an outcome.
