<p class="tldr"><strong>The short answer:</strong> most US small businesses pay a freelance social media manager a monthly retainer of roughly $500 to $2,500. Hourly rates run from about $14 on freelance marketplaces to $150 or more for senior strategists. Where you land depends on how many platforms you need, how much content, and whether design, writing and video are included. Ad spend sits on top of all of it.</p>

Money comes up on almost every intro call I have with a US business owner, usually near the end and a little sheepishly. There's nothing awkward about it. Asking what something costs before you commit is just good management, so here is the straight version, including the parts that sometimes get left off a proposal.

## How much does a freelance social media manager cost per month?

Most freelancers charge a flat monthly retainer, and most US small businesses see quotes somewhere between $500 and $2,500. Ask five freelancers and you'll get five different numbers. Pricing guides published in 2025 and 2026 don't agree with each other either, but they cluster into three rough bands:

| What you're buying | Typical monthly fee (USD) | What it usually looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduling and light posting | $300 to $800 | Posting content you mostly supply, on one or two platforms |
| Done-for-you content | $750 to $2,500 | Planning, design and captions on two or three platforms |
| Content plus extras | $2,500+ | Adds strategy, video editing, newsletters, community management or reporting |

Please read these as a ballpark, not a price list. Most published figures come from vendor blogs and marketplaces, and none of them is an official benchmark. A boutique bakery that wants four polished Reels a week is a completely different job from an HVAC company that wants one useful maintenance tip every Tuesday.

## What are typical freelance social media manager rates per hour?

Hourly rates range from about $14 to $150 or more, depending on experience and where you hire. The two published guides I checked on October 23, 2026 give these figures:

| Experience level | Hourly rate (USD) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Typical marketplace freelancer | $14 to $35 | [Upwork's hiring guide](https://www.upwork.com/hire/social-media-managers/cost/) |
| Mid-level, 2 to 4 years | $35 to $75 | [SolidGigs rate guide](https://solidgigs.com/blog/freelance-social-media-manager-rates/) |
| Expert, 5+ years | $75 to $150+ | [SolidGigs rate guide](https://solidgigs.com/blog/freelance-social-media-manager-rates/) |

The marketplace figure is pulled down by a global pool of freelancers competing on price. SolidGigs also puts basic retainers at around $750 a month and up. These are vendor-published numbers, so use them to sense-check a quote rather than to set a budget. Many freelancers bill monthly rather than hourly, which makes quotes easier to compare, and [consultants who set strategy rather than run accounts](../social-media-consultant-vs-manager-us/) tend to price differently again.

If you're weighing a freelancer against hiring a full-time employee, the Bureau of Labor Statistics doesn't publish a "social media manager" line. Its closest category, public relations specialists, had a [median annual wage of $74,750](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/public-relations-specialists.htm) in May 2025, before benefits, payroll taxes and software. Treat that as a rough proxy, not a like-for-like comparison.

## Why do social media management quotes vary so much?

Usually because the quotes aren't for the same work. "Social media management" can mean "I'll hit publish on the posts you send me" or "I'll plan your month, design every graphic, write every caption and answer your DMs."

These are the things that move the price most:

- **Who creates the content.** Supplying your own photos and copy keeps the fee down. When the freelancer designs and writes everything, you're paying for two skill sets.
- **How many platforms.** Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn each want different formats and a different tone. Adding one is never a simple copy and paste.
- **Video.** Reels and short-form video take far longer to make than a static post. Check whether they're in the package or priced per video.
- **Community management.** Replying to comments and messages every day is a real time commitment, and plenty of packages leave it out.
- **Reporting.** A one-page monthly summary is useful. A 20-slide deck nobody opens is a cost you're paying for.
- **Your industry.** Regulated fields such as finance and healthcare need extra care and an approval step.

## Is it cheaper to hire a freelancer or an agency?

For most small businesses, yes: a freelancer is usually the cheaper option and often the better fit. You talk directly to the person doing the work, so nothing gets lost in a hand-off, and you aren't funding an office and several layers of account management. For comparison, [MySocial's pricing guide](https://mysocial.io/blog/social-media-management-pricing-guide/), citing a 2025 Cloud Campaign roundup, puts agency "growth" packages at $2,000 to $5,000 a month and full-service retainers at $5,000 to $15,000 or more.

An agency starts to make sense when you need a lot happening at once: paid ads across several networks, video shoots, influencer campaigns, PR. If that's your situation, an agency can coordinate it. If what you need is a feed that looks consistent and posts that go out on schedule, a good freelancer will usually deliver that for less.

## What hidden costs should you ask about before you sign?

Cheap packages tend to get expensive in the details. Before you agree to anything, ask:

1. **How many revisions are included?** Two rounds per piece is common. "Unlimited" often means slow.
2. **Is ad spend included?** Almost never. Boosted posts and ads are paid directly to Meta, LinkedIn or whichever platform runs them, and that budget is separate from the management fee.
3. **Who pays for tools, stock images and fonts?** Scheduling software, design subscriptions and licensed photos can add a noticeable amount each month. Find out whether they're bundled or billed back to you.
4. **What does the contract say about ending it?** A month-to-month agreement with 30 days' notice is fair for a small business. A 12-month lock-in is a lot to ask before you've seen the work.
5. **Who owns the files?** You should. Templates and brand assets should be built in accounts you own, so they stay with you if you part ways.
6. **Who has the passwords?** Your accounts should stay in your name, with the freelancer added as a user or manager. Never hand over ownership of a business page.

## What does good value look like?

The lowest quote is rarely the best value, and the highest isn't automatically the strongest work. What your money should buy:

- **Consistency.** Content that goes out when it's supposed to, week after week, without you chasing anyone.
- **Your brand, done properly.** Posts that look and sound like your business, rather than a template a hundred other accounts are using.
- **Your evenings back.** Every hour you spend wrestling with design apps after closing is an hour you're not spending on customers.
- **A plan you can see.** You should know what's going out next month before it goes out.

Be wary of anyone who guarantees follower counts, leads or sales. Results depend on your product, pricing, market and budget, and no freelancer controls all of those. A good one can promise strong content, delivered on time, and honest reporting on how it performs.
